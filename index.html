Good to know · 常见问题

问 · 答

The questions I get most weeks.

Do I need a Chinese visa?

Most foreign passport holders need a tourist visa (L) to enter mainland China. Many nationalities also qualify for the new 240-hour visa-free transit through major entry cities — including Beijing, Shanghai and Chengdu. I'll check your passport against the current list and advise. Tibet requires an additional permit, which I arrange.

Can I pay in USD, EUR or GBP?

I quote in RMB but accept bank transfers in USD, EUR or GBP via Wise or HSBC at the daily mid-market rate. PayPal is available on request (small fee). A 20 % deposit secures your dates; the balance is due 30 days before departure.

Are flights and trains included in the price?

Domestic transport between cities — high-speed trains, internal flights, private cars — is included in every package price. International flights to and from China are not. I'll happily recommend routes, airlines and timing, but you book those yourself.

What if I have dietary needs or allergies?

Tell me when you inquire — kosher, halal, vegetarian, vegan, gluten-free, nut allergies, anything. China is far more accommodating than the guidebook suggests, once you know where to look. I do. Every menu I book is approved against your list.

How small are the groups, really?

For private trips, I cap at 6 travelers — that's firm. For family reunions or company retreats up to 12, I co-lead with a second trusted guide. Anything larger and we plan parallel tracks so nobody is stuck in a herd.

Tibet — what about altitude and permits?

Foreigners need both a China visa and a separate Tibet Travel Permit (TTP). I handle the TTP through licensed Lhasa agencies — you send passport scans 30 days in advance. Everest BC and Mt. Kailash need extra permits, also arranged by me. For altitude, we start at Lhasa (3,650 m) with a slow first day, and I keep oxygen and Diamox on hand.

Are kids welcome?

Absolutely — I've hosted travelers from age 4 to 81. Family trips get a slower morning, more snacks, and at least one stop a day that a 7-year-old will actually remember (panda-spotting, Kung Fu class, making dumplings with my mother).

What about cancellations?

Deposits are fully refundable up to 60 days before departure, and 50 % refundable up to 30 days before. After that, it depends on what's already booked (Tibet permits and small-hotel nights are often non-refundable). Full terms come with the quote.
